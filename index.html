About BAYSM

BAYSM 2026 is the 10th edition of the Bayesian Young Statisticians Meeting: the official conference of j-ISBA, the junior section of the International Society for Bayesian Analysis (ISBA). The meeting is held in person in even-numbered years, typically in conjunction with the ISBA World Meeting, and online in odd-numbered years.

BAYSM is a platform for connection within the Bayesian community. The conference provides many opportunities for students and early career researchers to meet with established senior researchers and to build their professional networks. Research across the many domains where Bayesian methods are employed is promoted.

BAYSM 2026 will be held in June 26 - 27, 2026 at Chiba University, Chiba, Japan, just prior to the 2026 ISBA world meeting.

All attendees are expected to adhere to the ISBA code of conduct.

Information about previous BAYSM editions can be found here.

Nianqiao Ju

Assistant Professor of Mathematics
Dartmouth College

Title: Recent Advances in Bayesian Inference from Privatized Data via Data Augmentation

Abstract: Analyzing data under privacy constraints is like dancing with shackles, as privacy protection requires modified analysis procedures and inevitably reduces data utility. Performing statistical inference on privatized data is even more challenging, as it requires deriving reliable insights from lower-quality data. Drawing on three recent studies, this talk presents a data augmentation Markov Chain Monte Carlo framework for Bayesian inference from privatized data. The core idea is to treat the unobserved confidential data as latent variables. We discuss an independent Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler and a recent algorithmic improvement. We present spectral gap bounds of reversible hybrid Gibbs samplers, validating the convergence of our data augmentation algorithms. The talk concludes with a discussion of some open problems in the field.

Beniamino Hadj-Amar

Assistant Professor
University of South Carolina

Title: Frequency Selection in Bayesian Spectral Modeling of Time Series Data with Applications to Wearable Device Measurements

Abstract: This work introduces a Bayesian spike-and-slab framework for spectral analysis of time series data. The proposed method combines frequency selection and dimensionality reduction with a refined grid of candidate frequencies, enabling high-resolution recovery of oscillatory components while promoting sparsity through a structured spike-and-slab prior. A stochastic search algorithm efficiently explores the posterior space, yielding posterior inclusion probabilities that quantify the relevance of each frequency. We extend the framework to multivariate signals via a hierarchical prior on frequency inclusion patterns, allowing the model to capture both shared and component-specific rhythms across multiple timeseries. Extensive simulation studies demonstrate the method’s robustness and superior performance in frequency estimation and spectral power reconstruction compared to existing approaches. Applied to actigraphy data from individuals with partial-onset seizures, the univariate model identifies clinically relevant circadian and ultradian rhythms. In a second application, for the joint analysis of physical activity and skin temperature from a healthy individual, the multivariate model reveals partially overlapping rhythmic components consistent with known physiological coupling. This work establishes a powerful and interpretable approach to spectral analysis, with broad applicability to wearable data, chronobiology, and personalized health monitoring.

Geoff Pleiss

Assistant Professor
University of British Columbia

Title: We Still Don't Understand High Dimensional Bayesian Optimization

Abstract: Bayesian optimization suffers in theory from a curse of dimensionality: the number of observations needed to optimize a black-box function growing exponentially. However, recent methods that assume smoothness or locality have made progress on problems with thousands of dimensions, well beyond what prior theory suggests is feasible. This talk discusses recent work characterizing why the curse of dimensionality may not hold in practice and new questions that emerge from this analysis. I will begin with a preliminary theoretical result characterizing a "good enough" regime of Bayesian optimization, in which coarse approximate optima can be found in polynomial time under mild smoothness or locality assumptions. The degree of the polynomial depends on the desired degree of exactness, reverting to an exponential bound for near-perfect solutions. The second half turns to a puzzling empirical finding: not only can approximate solutions be found in polynomial time, they can also be found with simple models. We show that linear models can yield near-state-of-the-art optimization performance once a geometric pathology is corrected for, despite the true objective being highly nonlinear. Together, these results suggest the need to depart from long-held intuitions about high-dimensional Bayesian optimization.

Beatrice Franzolini

Assistant Professor
University of Milano-Bicocca

Title: Partially Exchangeable Partitions à la de Finetti: Finite and Infinite Characterizations

Abstract: We study partially exchangeable partitions in the sense of de Finetti, where invariance in law is imposed with respect to the subgroup of permutations preserving a given ordered reference partition. In both finite and infinite settings, we provide explicit characterizations of the associated partition laws, which remain valid even when de Finetti-type representations are unavailable, that is, when an underlying representation in terms of random probability measures need not exist. In particular, we prove that the multiset of counts given by the sizes of the intersections between the random partition and the reference partition is a minimal sufficient statistic for the class of partially exchangeable partition laws. We then characterize the corresponding partially exchangeable partition probability functions through symmetry and consistency conditions analogous to those of exchangeable partition probability functions. We further establish the equivalence with partially exchangeable sequences as well as with predictive laws, thereby yielding a partition-based description of these objects that does not rely on de Finetti-type representations. These results provide a general foundation for random partitions beyond the exchangeable setting. From a statistical modelling perspective, they also clarify the learning mechanisms underlying partition-based models and provide constructive tools for specifying new partially exchangeable priors without necessarily relying on de Finetti-type representations.

Francesco Denti

Assistant Professor
University of Padova

Title: Bayesian Multiresolution Functional Regression and Clustering via Self-Truncating Cumulative Shrinkage Processes

Abstract: We propose a Bayesian multiresolution framework for functional data analysis that captures varying levels of complexity at a local scale. As is commonly done, the model represents each function as a sum of increasingly flexible layers, with each layer defined by a basis expansion (e.g., B-splines) whose resolution increases with depth. Spike-and-slab Lasso priors enable data-driven activation of basis coefficients, allowing each layer to selectively operate on local regions of the domain and producing a principled separation between smooth and highly variable segments of the function. To enforce ordered shrinkage across layers, we introduce the Self-Truncating Cumulative Shrinkage Process (stCUSP), a prior that regulates the effective number of active layers. stCUSP extends the original Cumulative Shrinkage Process by incorporating a probabilistic self-truncation mechanism that penalizes redundant layers via spike-and-slab distributions applied to the stick-breaking variables. This promotes parsimony and interpretability while retaining the adaptive shrinkage behavior characteristic of CUSP. We plan to extend the prior to a partially exchangeable framework, enabling nested clustering of functions according to their complexity, interpreted as the number of activated layers.

Jun Yang

Assistant Professor
University of Cophenagen

Title: Sub-Cauchy Sampling: Escaping the Dark Side of the Moon

Abstract: We introduce a Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm based on Sub-Cauchy Projection, a geometric transformation that generalizes stereographic projection by mapping Euclidean space into a spherical cap of a hyper-sphere, referred to as the complement of the dark side of the moon. We prove that our proposed method is uniformly ergodic for sub-Cauchy targets, namely targets whose tails are at most as heavy as a multidimensional Cauchy distribution, and show empirically its performance for challenging high-dimensional problems. The simplicity and broad applicability of our approach open new opportunities for Bayesian modeling and computation with heavy-tailed distributions in settings where most existing methods are unreliable.

Kerrie Mengersen

Kerrie Mengersen

Distinguished Research Professor in Statistical Science
Queensland University of Technology (QUT)

Title: Bayes in Practice: A Bayesian Cancer Atlas

Abstract: From its earliest beginnings, Bayesian statistics has been a synthesis of theory, methodology, computation and application. In this presentation, I will reflect on the development of the award-winning Australian Cancer Atlas (https://atlas.cancer.org.au/) and spotlight its Bayesian foundations. I will highlight some of the challenges and proposed solutions to modelling and visualisation of an awkward spatial geography, “filling in” missing covariates, and communicating uncertainty. I will also touch on new research directions inspired by the Atlas: new spatio-temporal models, spatial vulnerability indices, meta-analysis transfer learning, distributed AI and responsible data science. Importantly, these methodological discussions will be complemented by reflections on the impact of the work for patients, health practitioners, cancer support groups and government agencies. Bayesian statistics really can make a difference!

Title: Scaling Limits of Piecewise Deterministic Monte Carlo

Abstract: Piecewise deterministic Monte Carlo methods such as the Zig-Zag sampler, the Bouncy Particle Sampler, and Forward Event-Chain Monte Carlo provide a non-reversible, continuous-time alternative to classical MCMC. In this talk, I will review recent progress on their scaling limits, including high-dimensional Gaussian regimes, anisotropic targets, and transient fluid-limit descriptions. The emphasis will be on how weak convergence and generator-based analysis reveal the effective dynamics governing these algorithms, and on the open problems that remain for more complex target geometries.
